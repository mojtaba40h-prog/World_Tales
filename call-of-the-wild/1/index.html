Into the Primitive

Chapter summary

Buck lived at a spacious estate in the sun-kissed Santa Clara Valley, known as Judge Miller’s place. Here, he reigned supreme over a vast domain of rolling lawns, orchards, and stables. Buck was neither a house dog nor a kennel dog; the whole realm was his.

Born to a powerful St. Bernard father and a Scottish shepherd mother, he possessed dignity, strength, and intelligence. For four years, he lived a life of luxury and contentment, carrying the Judge’s grandchildren on his back and resting beside the fireplace during cool autumn evenings.

However, in the fall of 1897, the discovery of gold in the Klondike changed everything. Men from all over the world flooded into the frozen North, demanding heavy, strong dogs with thick coats to work as sled animals. Buck’s comfortable life came to a sudden end through the betrayal of Manuel, one of the Judge’s assistant gardeners.

Driven by gambling debts, Manuel kidnapped Buck during an evening stroll and delivered him to a stranger at a railway station. Buck was beaten, choked with a thick rope, and locked inside a wooden crate. Shipped north by train and boat, he suffered for days without food or water.

His rage grew steadily until he was finally released before a man dressed in a red sweater. Furious, Buck charged, only to be repeatedly struck down by a heavy wooden club. Through this painful lesson, Buck learned the fundamental rule of his new existence: the law of club and fang.

He was beaten, but not broken; he understood that a man with a club was a master to be obeyed, though never loved.
