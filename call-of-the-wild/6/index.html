Chapter 6 of 7

For the Love of a Man

Chapter summary

As Buck recovered under John Thornton’s compassionate care during the spring, he experienced an emotion he had never known before: true, passionate love. While Judge Miller’s friendship had been formal and dignified, Thornton treated his dogs as if they were his own children. He spoke to Buck, held his head lovingly, and cared for his physical well-being.

In return, Buck offered complete devotion, watching Thornton’s every move and guarding him with unwavering loyalty. Buck demonstrated his intense loyalty on numerous dramatic occasions. When an arrogant gunman attacked Thornton in a saloon, Buck instantly leaped at the man’s throat to defend his master.

Later, when Thornton’s boat overturned in a roaring river, Buck dove into the churning rapids, risking his life to drag Thornton safely to shore. Buck’s legendary devotion became famous throughout the mining camps of the North. In Dawson City, Buck accomplished an astounding feat of strength out of love for Thornton.

A wealthy miner bet Thornton sixteen hundred dollars that Buck could not pull a sled loaded with one thousand pounds of flour. Driven by Thornton’s gentle encouragement—“If you love me, Buck”—Buck broke the frozen runners free from the snow and pulled the enormous load one hundred yards. The crowd cheered wildly, and Thornton wept openly as he knelt beside his miraculous dog.
