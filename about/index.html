The stories

Treasure Island

Author: Robert Louis Stevenson (1850–1894)

Original publication: 1883

About Robert Louis Stevenson

Robert Louis Stevenson was a Scottish novelist, poet and travel writer, born in Edinburgh. Treasure Island, published in 1883, made him internationally famous; he went on to write The Strange Case of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde and Kidnapped, and spent his last years in Samoa.

Alice’s Adventures in Wonderland

Author: Lewis Carroll (1832–1898)

Original publication: 1865

About Lewis Carroll

Lewis Carroll was the pen name of Charles Lutwidge Dodgson, an English writer, mathematician, photographer, and Anglican deacon born in 1832. He studied and taught mathematics at Christ Church, Oxford, where he led a quiet academic life. Carroll possessed a unique talent for wordplay, logic, and storytelling, which he used to entertain the children of his university colleagues. His most famous works, Alice’s Adventures in Wonderland (1865) and its sequel Through the Looking-Glass (1871), revolutionized children’s literature by introducing clever nonsense, imaginative fantasy, and humor instead of traditional moral lessons. His iconic characters and inventive language have made a lasting contribution to world literature, captivating readers of all ages for over a century. Carroll passed away in 1898, leaving behind a rich literary legacy.

The Call of the Wild

Author: Jack London (1876–1916)

Original publication: 1903

About Jack London

Jack London was born John Griffith Chaney in San Francisco in 1876. Raised in a working-class household, he began working at ten and later served as an oyster pirate, a fish patrol member, a seal-hunting sailor and a freight-train hobo, educating himself in public libraries. In 1897 he joined the Klondike Gold Rush; the search for gold brought him little, but the frozen Yukon and its sled dogs inspired his finest writing, including The Call of the Wild (1903) and White Fang (1906). One of the first American authors to win worldwide fame through magazine fiction, he published more than fifty books exploring survival, instinct and the clash between civilization and nature.
